# Shaft Misalignment & Coupling Diagnostics

## Overview & Fault Description
Shaft misalignment occurs when the centerlines of two coupled shafts (e.g. electric motor and pump/compressor) are not collinear. Misalignment is categorized into offset (parallel) misalignment, angular misalignment, or a combination of both.

## Diagnostic Evidence & Sensor Signatures
- **2X & 1X Harmonics**: Strong vibration response at 2X shaft frequency, often accompanied by 1X and 3X running speed components.
- **Axial Vibration**: High axial vibration amplitude (especially in angular misalignment), often >50% of radial vibration.
- **Phase Shift**: 180-degree phase difference across the flexible coupling in axial or radial direction.
- **Coupling Temperature**: Elevated coupling guard temperature due to continuous flexing of coupling inserts.

## Root Causes
1. **Thermal Growth Unaccounted For**: Differential thermal expansion between driver motor and driven equipment during operating warm-up.
2. **Soft Foot Condition**: One or more motor feet not resting flat on the baseplate, distorting frame when bolted.
3. **Pipe Strain**: External forces exerted on pump/compressor casing by rigid piping expansion.

## Inspection & Remediation Protocol
1. **Soft Foot Correction**:
   - Loosen hold-down bolts one by one while monitoring dial indicator on each foot. Correct with stainless steel shims if deflection exceeds 0.05 mm (0.002 in).
2. **Laser Shaft Alignment**:
   - Mount laser alignment heads on motor and driven shafts.
   - Adjust motor position vertically with precision shims and horizontally with jacking bolts.
   - Achieve offset tolerance <0.05 mm and angularity <0.05 mm/100 mm.
3. **Coupling Inspection**: Inspect flexible elements (elastomer spiders, grid springs, gear teeth) for wear or cracking.
