# General Bearing Wear & Fatigue Degradation Guide

## Overview & Fault Description
General bearing wear refers to progressive material loss and raceway roughing caused by long-term operation, boundary lubrication conditions, or severe environmental contamination. Unlike localized defects, wear affects broad contact surfaces and leads to increased internal clearances and loss of running accuracy.

## Diagnostic Evidence & Sensor Signatures
- **Broadband Noise Floor**: Elevation of overall broadband vibration energy across 10 Hz – 10 kHz rather than sharp single-frequency peaks.
- **RMS Trend**: Monotonic upward slope in vibration velocity RMS and acceleration RMS over weeks of operation.
- **Temperature Rise**: Moderate, steady increase in bearing operating temperature (5°C to 15°C above baseline).
- **Acoustic Signature**: Continuous harsh rumbling sound.

## Root Causes
1. **Normal Operational Fatigue**: Exceeding calculated L10h bearing fatigue life under design loads.
2. **Fine Particle Abrasive Wear**: Fine dust or silica ingress acting as grinding compound.
3. **Inadequate Lubricant Film**: Viscosity drop due to high ambient operating temperatures or oil dilution.

## Maintenance & Monitoring Actions
1. **Clearance Measurement**: Check radial internal clearance (RIC) with feeler gauge or dial indicator; compare against ISO/DIN clearance tables (C2, Normal, C3).
2. **Vibration Severity Rating**: Evaluate overall RMS velocity against ISO 10816-3 standards (Class I, II, III industrial machinery).
3. **Action Plan**:
   - Re-lubricate immediately with correct viscosity grease.
   - Schedule bearing replacement during next scheduled machine shutdown if RMS velocity exceeds Zone C threshold (>4.5 mm/s RMS).
