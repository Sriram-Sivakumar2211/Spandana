# Rolling Element (Ball / Roller) Defect & Maintenance Procedure

## Overview & Fault Description
Rolling element faults involve damage to one or more balls or rollers within the bearing assembly, such as pitting, spalling, flat spots, or cracking. As a defective ball rotates, it impacts both the inner and outer raceways, producing characteristic vibration signatures at the Ball Spin Frequency (BSF) and Fundamental Train Frequency (FTF / Cage frequency).

## Diagnostic Evidence & Sensor Signatures
- **Vibration Frequencies**: Peaks at 2x BSF and 1x BSF, often modulated by FTF (cage frequency).
- **Harmonics & Sidebands**: Multiple BSF harmonics with cage speed sidebands. High spectral entropy and irregular peak patterns.
- **Noise Characteristics**: Irregular growling or knocking sound from the bearing housing.
- **Wavelet Energy**: Distributed high-energy bursts across upper frequency decomposition bands (`wavelet_energy_d1`, `wavelet_energy_d2`).

## Root Causes
1. **Skidding & Smearing**: Rapid acceleration under insufficient radial load causing rolling elements to skid across raceways.
2. **Solid Contamination**: Abrasive metallic or dirt particles trapped between balls and raceway.
3. **Over-greasing / Under-greasing**: Excessive grease causing ball churning and overheating, or dry operation causing scuffing.
4. **Current Fluting**: Electrical arcing across rolling elements creating cratering.

## Inspection & Remediation Protocol
1. **Cage & Ball Inspection**:
   - Dismantle bearing housing and inspect rolling elements under clean lighting. Look for dull spots, micro-cracks, or cage pocket wear.
   - Inspect cage pockets for fracture or excessive play.
2. **Debris & Grease Analysis**:
   - Sample grease for iron/steel particle counts and oxidation degradation.
3. **Corrective Action**:
   - Immediate or scheduled replacement of the complete bearing assembly. Never replace individual balls or rollers.
   - Clean lubricant reservoir and flush oil/grease lines.
